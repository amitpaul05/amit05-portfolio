Back on Instigate analytics. The same metric was coming out different on URL Distro, My Dashboard and Insights, so instead of picking one to trust I went looking for which one was lying. All three were. Story counters were being incremented from the client, and the stories_update_own policy quietly dropped every update that did not come from the owner. Nothing errored. Every play and complete from an actual audience had been thrown away since launch, and only the creator's own clicks ever landed. One story was showing 27 plays against 160 logged events.

So the counters are gone. media_events is the only source now, and the per-story views metric went with them, since it was counting the same play a second time.

Then the questions nobody had written down. Audience numbers leave out the creator's own activity. Authoring numbers keep it, because share, generate and paste can only be done by the creator anyway. A copied story credits the parent instead of the copy, so the number answers how often someone took my work. Per the data spec, stats cover public stories only, My Dashboard stays per account, and Insights is global and reads the same for every viewer, anonymous ones included.

I turned down two suggestions. Storing who did what would break the schema's rule against tracking people, so a boolean set server-side carries what we actually need. And a clamp on plays against completes would have hidden the mismatch rather than explained it, which is how we got here.

Growth then got rebuilt on real data. The sample-data fallback is deleted, each bucket holds its own value instead of a running total, bucket size follows the span of the data, empty buckets are filled, and the grid comes from the data. Review caught that one: the grid was still fixed at six by four, so eleven points were drawing as six.

A policy that failed loudly would have cost us an afternoon. This one returned success and dropped the write, and it cost us every audience number we had.
